Mental model

What's really happening under the hood

An LLM is a prediction engine. It reads your text and guesses the most likely next words — over and over.

  • It has no memory beyond the current conversation. Context you don't give, it doesn't have.
  • It's a brilliant improviser, not a database. It can sound confident and still be wrong ("hallucinate").
  • Your words steer the probabilities. More signal in = better prediction out.

🧭The one takeaway

Treat the model like a talented new hire on day one: capable and fast, but knows nothing about your situation unless you tell it. Brief it like you'd brief a smart colleague — goal, context, constraints, format.

The framework

Anatomy of a high-quality prompt

Six ingredients. You won't always need all six — but knowing them means you'll never stare at a blank box again.

1Role

Who should the AI be? "Act as a senior copy editor…"

2Task

The single, clear job. One verb: summarise, draft, compare, debug.

3Context

Background, audience, source material, what you've already tried.

4Examples

Show one or two samples of the style or format you want.

5Constraints

Length, tone, what to avoid, must-haves, reading level.

6Format

Output shape: table, bullet list, JSON, email, step-by-step.

Memory hook: R-T-C-E-C-F → "Role, Task, Context, Examples, Constraints, Format."

Fundamentals

Specific beats clever, every time

✗ VAGUE Write something about marketing.
✓ SPECIFIC # Role You are a B2B marketing strategist. # Task Write 3 LinkedIn post hooks # Context for a SaaS that helps clinics cut no-shows. Audience: clinic owners. # Constraints <20 words each, no jargon, end with a question.

The vague prompt could mean a thousand things — so you get the blandest average of all of them. Specificity removes guesswork.

Technique 1

Role & persona prompting

Assigning a role primes the model to pull from the right "voice" and depth of knowledge. "Explain like a kindergarten teacher" and "explain like a tax attorney" produce very different answers — by design.

  • Be specific about seniority. "Senior" or "expert" raises the rigour.
  • Add a perspective, not just a job. "…who is skeptical and asks hard questions."
  • Stack a target reader. Role + audience = controlled tone.
# Pro pattern You are a veteran UX researcher. Review my onboarding flow as if preparing a critique for a design review. Be direct, cite specific friction points, and rank them by user impact.
Technique 2

Context setting — the biggest lever of all

Most "bad AI answers" are really starved-of-context answers. The model can only work with what's in front of it.

🎯Goal

What does success look like? What's this for?

👤Audience

Who reads the output — and what do they already know?

📄Source

Paste the real data, notes, or document. Don't make it guess.

Pro tip: end with "Ask me up to 3 questions before you start if anything is unclear." Turns a one-shot guess into a briefing.

✍️ Practice Task 1 · 4 minutes

Rescue a lazy prompt

Take this weak prompt and rewrite it using Role + Context + Constraints + Format:

"Give me ideas for my coffee shop."

Pick a real-ish scenario: who's the audience, what's the goal, what format do you want back? Type it into your AI tool and run it.

🔍 What good looks like · Reveal 1
✓ A strong rewrite You are a small-business marketing consultant. My context: an independent coffee shop near a university, quiet on weekday afternoons. Goal: drive 2–4pm weekday traffic. Give me 6 low-cost ideas, each with a one-line "why it works" and an estimated effort (low/med/high). Format as a table.

✅ Checklist — does yours have…

  • A clear role and a specific goal (not just "ideas")?
  • Context that narrows the problem (location, timing, audience)?
  • A defined output shape (count, columns, format)?
  • A constraint that filters quality (low-cost, effort rating)?

Common miss: people add a role but forget the goal — so the AI optimises for nothing in particular.

Technique 3

Few-shot examples — show, don't just tell

When tone or format is hard to describe, give 1–3 examples. The model pattern-matches your samples far better than it follows adjectives.

Rewrite product names into punchy taglines. Example 1 → "Wireless earbuds" → "Silence, summoned." Example 2 → "Standing desk" → "Work on your feet, not your back." Now do: "Insulated water bottle" → ?

Rule of thumb: 2 good examples usually beat a paragraph of instructions. Keep them consistent — the model copies your inconsistencies too.

Technique 4

Make it think step-by-step

For anything involving logic, math, planning, or multi-part analysis, ask the model to reason before it answers. Quality jumps because it doesn't blurt the first guess.

  • "Think step by step" — the classic trigger.
  • "Work through it, then give a final answer."
  • "List your assumptions first," then solve — catches bad premises early.

Don't need the reasoning shown? Add: "reason internally, then output only the final answer."

# Weak What's the best pricing tier? → a guess # Strong Step by step: list our cost per unit, compare 3 pricing models, weigh pros/cons, THEN recommend one with reasoning.
✍️ Practice Task 2 · 5 minutes

Teach the AI your style

Pick a small writing job (email subject lines, headlines, or short replies). Give the AI two examples in your preferred style, then ask it to produce three more — same voice.

🎯Goal

Get outputs that sound like you, not generic AI.

🧪Bonus

Add "step by step, first describe the pattern you see in my examples."

🔍 What good looks like · Reveal 2

Did the style actually transfer?

✅ Signs it worked

  • New outputs match your examples' length and rhythm.
  • Same tone (playful / formal / punchy) carried through.
  • It picked up structural quirks — e.g. always ending on a verb.

⚠️ Common mistakes

  • Inconsistent examples → the model averages them into mush.
  • Examples too similar → it can't tell what's essential vs incidental.
  • Forgot to say "same style" → it reverts to its default voice.

Fix-it move: when output drifts, reply with "More like example 2, less like example 1" — comparative feedback is gold.

Technique 5

Control the shape of the answer

Don't accept whatever the model feels like returning. Specify the format and you can paste it straight into a doc, sheet, or app.

# Ask for a table Compare these 3 laptops in a markdown table: columns = Price, Battery, Weight, Best for. One row each.
# Ask for JSON (great for automation) Return ONLY valid JSON: {"title":"", "tags":[], "summary":""} No prose, no markdown fences.

Formats worth knowing: Markdown tableNumbered stepsJSONCSVPros / ConsEmail draft

Technique 6

Constraints make outputs usable

📏Length & depth

"In exactly 5 bullets." "Under 100 words." "One paragraph, then a TL;DR."

🎚️Tone & reading level

"Friendly but professional." "Explain so a 12-year-old gets it." "No buzzwords."

🚫Negative constraints

"Don't use the word 'leverage'." "No emojis." "Avoid hedging — commit to a recommendation."

✅ Must-haves

"Every claim needs a reason." "Include one counter-argument." "Cite which input you used."

Counter-intuitive truth: tighter constraints raise quality. Freedom produces vagueness; limits force decisions.

Technique 7

System vs. user prompts — the standing orders

Many tools let you set a system prompt (or "custom instructions" / "project rules"): persistent rules that apply to every message, so you stop repeating yourself.

  • System = who the AI is + standing rules ("Always answer in British English, be concise, never invent facts").
  • User = the specific request for this turn.
  • Set persona, tone, and guardrails once at the system level; keep per-message prompts short.
# System (set once) You are my writing assistant. Always: concise, active voice, British spelling, no clichés. If unsure, ask before guessing. # User (each time) Draft a reply to this email: …
Technique 8

Dial creativity & length on purpose

Even without technical settings, you can steer the model's "temperature" and verbosity with words.

🎨More creative / divergent

"Give me 10 wildly different angles, including unconventional ones." "Brainstorm freely — quantity over polish."

🎯More precise / deterministic

"Be factual and conservative." "Only state what you're confident about." "One best answer, no alternatives."

📐Control verbosity

"One-line answer." vs "Go deep — assume I'm technical and want detail."

🔁Vary the seed

Hate the result? "Give me 3 completely different versions" beats re-rolling the same one.

✍️ Practice Task 3 · 5 minutes

Make the AI fill a real table

Think of a decision you're weighing (tools, trips, purchases, vendors). Ask the AI to build a comparison table with columns you choose — and a final recommendation row with reasoning.

Starter "Compare [A], [B], [C] as a markdown table. Columns: Cost, Learning curve, Best for. Then recommend one for [my situation], step by step."
🔍 What good looks like · Reveal 3

Grading your structured output

✅ A strong result has…

  • A clean table you could paste anywhere — consistent columns, no rambling.
  • Cells that are comparable (same unit / scale across rows).
  • A reasoned recommendation, not just "it depends".
  • It respected your columns exactly — didn't invent extra ones.
# If it ignored your format, anchor harder: "Output ONLY the table, nothing before or after. Exactly these columns: …"

Reliability tip: for automation/JSON, add "If a value is unknown, use null — never make one up." This is how you stop silent hallucinations.

Technique 9

Treat it as a conversation, not a vending machine

The first answer is a draft. Your real skill is the follow-up. Refine instead of restarting.

1Draft

Get a first version, however rough.

2Diagnose

"Too formal." "Section 2 is weak." "Add data."

3Direct

Give a precise next instruction, not "make it better".

4Repeat

2–3 rounds usually gets you to great.

Power moves: "What would make this stronger?" · "Critique your own answer, then improve it." · "Keep everything except the intro — rewrite just that."

Troubleshooting

Common failure modes & the fix

SymptomWhy it happensThe fix
Confident but wrong (hallucination)It's predicting, not looking up"Only use the text I gave you. Say 'not stated' if missing."
Generic, bland answerPrompt was too openAdd role, audience, constraints, an example
Ignores part of your requestToo many instructions at onceNumber them; or split into steps
Wrong formatFormat stated loosely"Output ONLY a table with these exact columns"
Too long / wafflyNo length constraint"Max 120 words. Lead with the answer."

Golden rule: if the output is bad, your prompt is the bug. Fix the brief, not your expectations.

Part 2 · The tools tour

The map: pick the right tool for the job

There are thousands of AI tools. You don't need them all — you need one good one per category. Here's how the landscape breaks down.

💬Chat & reasoning

Your everyday thinking partner.

✍️Writing

Drafting, editing, repurposing.

💻Coding

Build & debug, even as a non-coder.

🎨Image · audio · video

Create media from text.

🔎Research

Search with sources & synthesis.

⚙️Automation

Connect AI to your workflows.

We'll name well-known options as examples — the category and what it's best for matters more than any single brand.

Category 1

Chat assistants & reasoning models

💬General assistants

ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini. Best for: writing, analysis, brainstorming, learning, everyday Q&A.

🧠"Reasoning" modes

Deeper-thinking variants for hard logic, math, and multi-step planning. Slower, but far more reliable on complex problems.

📎Bring your files

Most now accept PDFs, images, and spreadsheets. Upload the source instead of describing it.

Pro tip: keep two assistants open and compare on important tasks — different models have different strengths and blind spots.

Category 2

Writing & content tools

✏️Editing & polish

Grammarly, general LLMs. Best for: grammar, tone shifts, tightening, adapting one piece for many channels.

📰Long-form & SEO

Jasper, Copy.ai, Notion AI. Best for: templated marketing content, outlines, repurposing at volume.

🔁Repurposing

Turn one webinar into a blog, 5 social posts, and an email. "Repurpose this into X for Y audience."

🗣️Voice & brand

Feed the tool 3 samples of your writing → it mirrors your voice. The few-shot trick from earlier.

Category 3

Coding assistants — even if you don't code

⌨️In-editor

GitHub Copilot, Cursor. Autocomplete and whole-function generation right inside your code.

🧩Build-by-chatting

Claude, ChatGPT, v0, Replit. Describe an app or script; get working code you can run.

🐞Debug & explain

Paste an error → "Explain this and fix it." Brilliant for learning, not just fixing.

Non-coder superpower: "Write me a small script to rename / sort / clean these files" — automate boring tasks without learning to program.

Category 4

Image, audio & video generation

🖼️Images

Midjourney, DAL·E, Adobe Firefly. Concepts, illustrations, mockups, social graphics.

🎙️Voice & audio

ElevenLabs. Natural voiceovers, narration, dubbing in many languages.

🎬Video

Runway, Pika, Sora-class tools. Short clips, b-roll, animation from text.

📝Meetings

Otter, Fireflies. Auto transcripts, summaries, action items.

Image prompting mirrors text prompting: subject + style + lighting + composition + mood. "A cozy café, watercolor style, warm afternoon light, wide shot."

Category 5

Research & knowledge tools

🔎Answer engines

Perplexity, Gemini, ChatGPT search. Best for: questions that need current info with citations you can verify.

📚Chat with documents

NotebookLM. Upload your sources; ask questions grounded only in them. Great for study & reports.

🧾Deep research modes

Agents that browse many sources and return a structured, cited brief. Use for serious due diligence.

Always-verify rule: treat AI research as a fast first draft, not a final source. Click the citations before you trust a number.

Category 6

Productivity & automation

⚙️Connect everything

Zapier, Make. Trigger AI steps inside workflows — "summarise every new form response and email it to me."

🗂️Workspace AI

Notion, Microsoft Copilot, Google Gemini in Workspace. AI living inside your docs, sheets, inbox.

🤖Custom assistants

Build a reusable "GPT"/"Project" with your instructions + files baked in. Set it up once, reuse forever.

The mindset shift: stop doing repetitive tasks manually. If you've done it 3 times, ask "can AI do this on a trigger?"

🔍 Self-assessment · Reveal 4

Score your real-world prompt

✅ A great prompt scores 4/4

  • You'd be happy to send the output with light edits.
  • It needed one refinement round or fewer.
  • You gave real context, not a generic description.
  • You picked the right tool for the job.

⚠️ If it fell flat…

  • Probably under-briefed — add audience & goal.
  • Wrong tool — e.g. asked a chat model for current facts (use a research tool).
  • Tried to do too much in one prompt — break it into steps.

Share-out: who got a result they'd actually use? What changed it from "meh" to "useful"?

Pro tier · Part 1

Lesser-known tricks that punch above their weight

🔄Make AI write the prompt

"I want to achieve X. Ask me questions, then write the ideal prompt for me." Meta-prompting beats guessing.

🧐Self-critique loop

"Now critique that answer for weaknesses, then rewrite it addressing them." One message, two-stage quality boost.

🎭Multiple personas

"Respond as an optimist, then a skeptic, then synthesise." Surfaces blind spots in seconds.

🪜Prompt chaining

Break big jobs into steps: outline → draft → edit. Each step's output feeds the next, with checkpoints.

Pro tier · Part 2

More tweaks the pros rely on

🚪Give it an "out"

"If you're not sure, say so." A simple line that dramatically cuts confident-but-wrong answers.

📥Pre-load the format

Start the answer for it: "Begin your reply with: 'Summary:'…" The model continues your pattern.

🔢Constrain quantity precisely

"Exactly 5, no more." Models drift on "a few" — give a number.

🧱Delimit your inputs

Wrap pasted text in """triple quotes""" or tags so the model never confuses instructions with data.

Bonus: keep a personal prompt library. Your best prompts are reusable assets — save the winners.

🔍 Capstone rubric · Reveal 5

What a reusable assistant should have

# Example: Meeting → Actions assistant Role: You are my executive assistant. Task: Turn raw meeting notes into actions. Rules: Output 3 sections — 1) TL;DR (2 lines) 2) Decisions made 3) Action items: owner, task, due date If an owner/date is missing, mark "TBD". Then: review your list for anything vague and tighten it. Input: """[paste notes]"""

✅ Score it

  • Could a teammate reuse it unchanged next week?
  • Is the output format fixed and paste-ready?
  • Does it handle missing info gracefully (TBD / "not stated")?
  • Did you add a quality step (self-check, steps, or examples)?
The whole workshop in one slide

Seven things to remember

  • Brief it like a smart new hire — goal, context, constraints, format.
  • Specific beats clever. Vagueness in, vagueness out.
  • Show examples when words can't capture the style.
  • Ask for reasoning on anything complex.
  • Control the format so output is instantly usable.
  • Iterate — the follow-up is where quality lives.
  • If it's bad, fix the prompt, then save the winners.

The meta-skill: clear thinking. A good prompt is just a clearly-articulated request — which makes you better at communicating with humans too.
