What is Artificial Intelligence?

AI is software that can perform tasks that normally require human-like thinking.

🗣

Language

AI can understand and generate text, explanations, questions and summaries.

👁

Vision

AI can help read images, slides, scanned pages and visual material.

📊

Patterns

AI can find patterns in data, text, feedback, marks and research notes.

🤖

Assistance

AI can act like a draft assistant, study partner, reviewer or idea generator.

Simple Analogy

AI is like a very fast assistant, not an automatic authority

It can help you draft, organize, explain and compare, but it does not replace human judgment.

Good use

Ask AI to help you understand, structure, improve, practice and review your work.

Bad use

Copy AI output without checking facts, sources, originality, policy and context.

✅

You decide

Teacher, student or researcher remains responsible.

🔍

You verify

Check claims, references, calculations and examples.

✍

You finalize

Use AI drafts as starting points, not final submissions.

What AI Is Not

AI is not a mind reader, subject expert or policy owner

Clear input and human review are still required.

❌

Not always accurate

AI can invent facts, citations, numbers or confident explanations.

❌

Not your institution policy

Academic integrity rules still come from your department or institution.

❌

Not a substitute for learning

Students should use AI to practice and understand, not to avoid thinking.

❌

Not safe for secrets

Do not paste private, confidential or sensitive data into public tools.

Academic Integrity

Use AI transparently, ethically and according to rules

Every institution may have different policies, so local policy comes first.

🧾

Declare when required

Follow course, department and journal rules for disclosure.

🧠

Keep your thinking visible

Show notes, reasoning, drafts, calculations or process when needed.

🔐

Protect privacy

Do not upload student records, exams, unpublished data or confidential files.

📌

Cite real sources

AI is not a source. Cite books, papers, datasets and official documents.

Prompt Engineering

Prompt engineering means asking clearly

A prompt is the instruction you give to AI. Better instructions create better outputs.

Role + Task + Context + Format + Limits + Check
🎭

Role

Tell AI what role to play: tutor, reviewer, editor, researcher or planner.

🎯

Task

State the exact result you need.

📦

Context

Give audience, level, subject, source text and background.

📐

Format

Request table, checklist, lesson plan, quiz, email or summary.

Prompt Formula

The complete academic prompt formula

Use this structure for most teaching, learning and research tasks.

1

Role

Act as a supportive university tutor / teacher / research assistant.

2

Task

Create, explain, review, summarize, compare or improve something specific.

3

Context

Mention subject, level, department, audience and source material.

4

Format

Ask for bullets, table, rubric, lesson plan, quiz or one-page brief.

5

Limits

Set length, tone, difficulty, citations, privacy and no fake facts.

6

Check

Ask AI to list assumptions, missing information and risks.

Bad vs Good Prompt

Small prompt changes create big quality changes

A good prompt gives purpose, audience, source and output format.

Weak Prompt

Explain photosynthesis.

Better Prompt

Act as a biology teacher. Explain photosynthesis for first-year students using a simple analogy, a labeled step list, 3 common mistakes and 5 quiz questions with answers.

Practice Task

Improve a weak prompt

6 minutes
  • Write one weak prompt from your subject.
  • Add role, audience, format and check step.
  • Compare the output before and after.
Prompt Pattern 1

Ask AI to teach like a tutor

Use this when students struggle with a concept.

Tutor PromptAct as a patient tutor. Explain [topic] to a [level] student. Use simple language, one analogy, two examples, three common mistakes and five practice questions. Do not skip steps.
Practice Task

Tutor prompt practice

7 minutes
  • Replace [topic] with a real topic from your class.
  • Run the prompt.
  • Ask a student or colleague which part is clearest.
Prompt Pattern 2

Ask AI to make teaching material

Use this when teachers need classroom-ready structure.

Lesson PromptAct as a teacher. Create a 45-minute lesson plan on [topic] for [level]. Include learning outcomes, warm-up, explanation, activity, assessment, homework and required materials.
Practice Task

Lesson prompt practice

8 minutes
  • Use a topic from your department.
  • Ask AI to make the lesson easier for weak students.
  • Ask AI to add one active learning activity.
Prompt Pattern 3

Ask AI to review writing

Use this for essays, lab reports, proposals and assignments after a first draft exists.

Review PromptReview this draft for clarity, structure, argument, missing evidence, repetition, tone and academic integrity risks. Do not rewrite first. Give feedback in a table, then provide an improved version.
Practice Task

Review prompt practice

8 minutes
  • Paste a short non-sensitive paragraph.
  • Ask for feedback table first.
  • Improve the paragraph yourself after reading feedback.
Prompt Pattern 4

Ask AI to create assessment items

Use this to build practice questions, not to replace teacher judgment.

Assessment PromptCreate 10 assessment questions on [topic] for [level]. Include 4 MCQs, 3 short questions and 3 application questions. Provide answers, difficulty level and the learning outcome tested.
Practice Task

Assessment prompt practice

7 minutes
  • Create questions for one topic.
  • Check whether each question matches your learning outcome.
  • Remove or revise weak questions manually.
Iterative Prompting

The first answer is rarely the final answer

Good AI users improve output through follow-up prompts.

1

First ask

Get a rough answer or structure.

2

Narrow

Ask AI to simplify, shorten, make practical or change audience level.

3

Verify

Ask what assumptions, missing information and possible errors exist.

4

Improve

Ask for a clearer version, better examples or table format.

5

Finalize

Human checks and edits before sharing or submitting.

Practice Task

Two-round improvement

6 minutes
  • Run one prompt.
  • Then ask: “Make it more practical and add a verification checklist.”
  • Compare the two outputs.

Do Not Paste

Academic data that should not go into public AI tools

Use anonymized or sample data unless your institution has approved a secure tool.

🔐

Student records

Marks, IDs, attendance, disciplinary data or private feedback.

🧪

Unpublished research

Raw data, confidential methods, grant drafts or reviewer comments.

📝

Exam material

Unreleased questions, answer keys or controlled assessment files.

🏥

Sensitive data

Medical, legal, financial or personal information.

Verification Checklist

Before you use AI output, check these five things

This is the minimum human review step.

1

Accuracy

Are facts, terms, calculations and examples correct?

2

Sources

Are claims supported by real, reliable sources?

3

Originality

Is the final work your own and policy-compliant?

4

Privacy

Did you remove sensitive or confidential information?

5

Fitness

Is the output suitable for audience, level and purpose?

Learn it the same way people learned smartphones: start using it, make small mistakes safely, improve your habits and keep human judgment in control.
