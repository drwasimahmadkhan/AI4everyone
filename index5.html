Foundation

What is Artificial Intelligence?

AI is software that can perform tasks that usually need human-like thinking, language understanding, pattern recognition and decision support.

🗣

Language

Write, rewrite, explain, translate and summarize.

📊

Patterns

Find signals in marks, surveys, notes and research data.

👁

Vision

Read charts, scanned pages, diagrams and images.

🧠

Reasoning Support

Break a problem into steps and compare possible options.

Important Mindset

AI is a Co-Pilot, Not the Pilot

The best academic use of AI is human-led: the person sets the goal, AI supports the work, and the person verifies the result.

AI can accelerate work, but it cannot replace academic judgment.
✅

Useful for drafts

Ideas, outlines, summaries, examples and structure.

✅

Useful for review

Clarity, gaps, tone, questions and improvement.

⚠

Not final authority

Grades, clinical choices, research claims and policy decisions need human responsibility.

Limitations

What AI Cannot Do Reliably

AI can sound confident even when it is incomplete, outdated or wrong. Treat it like an assistant that needs checking.

❌

No guaranteed truth

Facts, references and numbers can be wrong.

❌

No hidden expertise

It may not understand local policy, lab context or clinical nuance.

❌

No ethics shortcut

Privacy, consent and academic integrity remain human responsibilities.

❌

No final verification

Important outputs must be checked from reliable sources.

Safe Use

The 5-Check Rule Before Using AI Output

Use this rule before sharing any academic, research or departmental output.

1

Accuracy

Names, dates, values, definitions and claims.

2

Evidence

Source, textbook, paper, policy or guideline.

3

Privacy

No personal, confidential or sensitive information exposed.

4

Originality

No plagiarism, fake references or blind copying.

5

Purpose

Output fits the audience and academic context.

Prompt Engineering

The Skill of Asking Well

Prompt engineering is the ability to give AI a clear task, useful context and the right output format.

Better input produces better output.
1

Role

The type of help expected from AI.

2

Task

The exact result that should be produced.

3

Context

The audience, level, background and purpose.

4

Format

Table, bullets, outline, checklist or summary.

Prompt Formula

A Simple Formula for Academic Work

This formula works for teaching, research, administration and student learning.

Role+Task+Context+Format+Checks
Role

Example: academic assistant

Defines the perspective.

Task

Example: summarize a paper

Defines the output.

Context

Example: second-year students

Defines the audience.

Checks

Accuracy, ethics, gaps

Improves reliability.

Prompt Quality

Weak Request vs Strong Request

Small changes in the request can make AI output more useful and easier to verify.

Weak

Explain pharmacology.

Stronger

Explain one pharmacology topic for second-year students using key concepts, a simple analogy, a table and review questions.

Prompt Loop

Do Not Stop at the First Answer

The first AI answer is usually a draft. A second review step often improves quality.

1

Draft

Generate a first version quickly.

2

Critique

Check clarity, missing points and risk.

3

Improve

Create a cleaner and more useful version.

4

Verify

Confirm facts, sources and suitability.
